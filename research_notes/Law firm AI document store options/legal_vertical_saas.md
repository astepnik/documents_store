## Key question 0: What does "Libra" most likely refer to?

### Takeaway
"Libra" almost certainly means **Libra by Wolters Kluwer** (formerly Libra Technology GmbH / Libra.ai, Berlin), a legal AI *workspace/assistant* for law firms in Germany and now Poland and other WK markets — not a document management system. It connects to a firm's SharePoint/OneDrive and to WK practice-management systems (Kleos, Legisway) rather than replacing them. A different, unrelated UK product at libra.law (Tabled Technologies) also exists.

### Cited Findings
- Libra (Berlin) is a "Legal AI Workspace" for law firms; described as ISO 27001-certified, GDPR-compliant and hosted in the EEA, with research across Wolters Kluwer and Otto Schmidt legal databases plus access to internal documents via SharePoint and OneDrive — [legal-tech.de directory entry](https://legal-tech.de/legal-tech-verzeichnis/eintrag/libra-ai/); [ki-company.ai test](https://www.ki-company.ai/en/blog-beitraege/libra-ai-put-to-the-test-legal-ai-workspace-for-law-firms)
- Founder named as Viktor von Essen; German sources describe a legal-specific model called Noxtua being used (note: Noxtua is also the name of the separate C.H. Beck/Xayn legal AI — see Q6; the exact relationship was not verifiable in this session) — [legal-tech.de KI-Tools 2026](https://legal-tech.de/zwoelf-ki-tools-fuer-kanzleien/)
- Wolters Kluwer Legal & Regulatory acquired Libra Technology GmbH for up to EUR 90m (EUR 30m upfront, remainder earn-out); announced 14 Nov 2025, completed 19 Nov 2025 — [JUVE](https://www.juve.de/markt-und-management/wolters-kluwer-uebernimmt-legal-tech-anbieter-libra/); [Wolters Kluwer completion release](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/news/wolters-kluwer-completes-acquisition-of-libra-technology-gmbh); [Libra journal post](https://libratech.ai/journal/libra-joins-wolters-kluwer)

- Feb 2026: WK announced launch of the Libra platform on the Polish market, combining generative AI with LEX content; Polish WK clients could use a free trial — [BusinessWire Feb 2026 (PL)](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260203925761/pl)
- Connectors per Libra docs FAQ: SharePoint and OneDrive for document access; Word and Outlook add-ins; Kleos, Legisway, NotaioNext and SuiteNext for case/matter files. A Libra "Database" can *link* a SharePoint folder instead of copying it and keeps the link in sync with the source — [Libra docs FAQ](https://docs.libratech.ai/en/resources/faq)

- The workspace licence does not include WK legal content: "After the trial, Wolters Kluwer access must be purchased separately" — [Libra docs FAQ](https://docs.libratech.ai/en/resources/faq)
- Unrelated namesake: libra.law is a UK (England & Wales) platform by Tabled Technologies Ltd that explains law in plain English, drafts from templates and connects consumers/businesses with firms; Professional tier GBP 299.99/year — [libra.law](https://libra.law/); [libra.law/platform/overview](https://libra.law/platform/overview)

### Inferences
- Libra is an AI assistant/workspace layer (research + drafting + chat over linked documents), not a system of record. For a 100 GB+ email/file-share archive it would sit on top of SharePoint/OneDrive or Kleos rather than hold the archive itself.
- Its SharePoint "linked Database" model implies the firm must first get its email and file-share content into SharePoint/OneDrive (or a WK PMS) in a client/matter structure; Libra does not appear to offer Exchange/PST or file-share ingestion itself.
- With WK ownership, Libra is the natural choice for firms already on WK content (Germany: Otto Schmidt/WK; Poland: LEX) and for Kleos users.

### Gaps
- No public documentation found on an API or MCP endpoint for Libra, on whether it builds a persistent vector index of linked documents, on supported index size, or on metadata (client/matter/handler) modelling. Exact 2026 price list could not be verified because libratech.ai could not be fetched.
- Whether Libra's model is the C.H. Beck/Xayn "Noxtua" or a differently named internal model could not be confirmed.

## Key question 1: Legal document management systems (system-of-record products)

### Takeaway
Two cloud DMSs dominate the "hold everything, indexed by client/matter" tier — NetDocuments (ndMAX, Smart Answers, Legal Context Graph, hosted MCP server; regions US/UK/DE/AU/CA) and iManage Work (Insight+, Ask iManage, MCP server GA May 2026; ~10 Azure regions, on-prem still sold). Both are quote-priced (~USD 50–75/user/month core, add-ons extra) with 3–10 user minimums. Practice-management suites (Clio, LEAP, Smokeball, Actionstep, Litify, Filevine, PracticePanther) include document storage plus a matter-scoped AI assistant but are built around their own matter record rather than as archival DMSs. Epona365 is the SharePoint-native alternative that keeps data in the firm's own M365 tenant.

### Inferences
- For a 100 GB+ heterogeneous archive (email + SharePoint + file shares) with client/handler/matter/case-type metadata and agent access, only NetDocuments and iManage (and, inside M365, Epona365) are architected as archival systems of record with email filing, rich profile metadata and vendor-run MCP servers. Practice-management suites store documents per matter but are not designed to absorb legacy archives at that scale and offer matter-scoped (not firm-wide) AI.
- Both big DMS vendors have made agent access a licensed feature: NetDocuments gates MCP behind ndMAX Enterprise (from 1 Apr 2026); iManage bundles Ask iManage at no extra cost but requires cloud (cloudimanage.com) for all AI.
- Minimum realistic firm size: NetDocuments 3–5 users; iManage ~10 users plus a dedicated DMS owner; practice-management suites from solo upward.

### Gaps
- Could not read NetDocuments or iManage documentation on the exact metadata model (cabinets/workspaces/profile attributes vs. iManage workspaces/custom fields) or on whether AI processing at ingest (classification, embeddings) is optional; the search budget ran out before those queries ran.
- No official price list for any DMS; implementation fees found only as third-party ranges (see Q5).
- No confirmation whether Insight+ indexes email bodies/attachments by default, or whether Ask iManage is available to on-prem Work 10 (one 2024 source says cloud-only).

## Key question 2: Legal AI knowledge/search layers (sit on top of an existing DMS/M365)

### Takeaway
Harvey, Legora, DeepJudge, CoCounsel Legal, Lexis+ Protégé/Create+ and Jylo all connect to iManage, NetDocuments and SharePoint (mostly via MCP or vendor connectors) and leave the DMS as system of record; they are quote-priced at roughly USD 200–2,400 per seat per month with 10–100 seat minimums (figures are third-party estimates). DeepJudge is the only one specialising in firm-wide knowledge *search* (with on-prem/private-cloud options); Robin AI wound down independent operations in Dec 2025–Jan 2026. None of these was found to offer bulk ingestion of raw Exchange/PST or file-share archives.

## Key question 7: Vendor lock-in and export (getting data and metadata out)

### Takeaway
NetDocuments provides a built-in web Export (ZIP with metadata, or metadata only) across all regions after retiring its Mass Export Utility in Mar/Apr 2026; iManage bulk export relies mainly on third-party tools (e.g., Esentio Power Exporter); in both cases metadata requires field-by-field remapping into the target system, and third-party migration services are a standard cost. Export from AI layers was not documented.

### Cited Findings
- NetDocuments web Export: GA in all regions; exports from searches, workspaces, CollabSpaces and folders into a ZIP, with metadata or metadata-only — [ndWeb Export Tool Available](https://support.netdocuments.com/s/article/ndWeb-Export-Tool-Available); legacy Mass Export Utility EOL EU region March 2026, US April 2026 — [EOL announcement](https://support.netdocuments.com/s/article/End-of-Life-Announcement-for-Legacy-Mass-Export-Tool)
- Legacy utility's DocumentList.xml carried client, matter, doctype, author and comments; "an export without a metadata file is not a true DMS export" (consultant) — [Optiable Mass Export method](https://www.optiable.com/how-to-exports-documents-of-netdocuments-method-1-mass-export-utility/); [Optiable switching firms](https://www.optiable.com/moving-to-a-new-firm-with-a-document-management-system/)
- ndMirror copies a whole cabinet locally incl. versions and a SQL metadata backup, but deletions in NetDocuments propagate to the mirror — [Optiable ndMirror](https://www.optiable.com/how-to-export-documents-out-of-netdocuments-method-3-ndmirror/)
- Third-party migration tooling exists (Universal Migrator "Migrate from NetDocuments") — [Universal Migrator](https://www.universalmigrator.com/migrate-from/netdocuments)
- iManage: Esentio Power Exporter selects metadata fields and outputs metadata in multiple formats; no native iManage bulk-export documentation found — [Esentio products](https://www.esentio.com/home-2/products/)
- Mapping into iManage requires one-by-one profile-attribute to workspace-metadata mapping — [Fusion Computing](https://fusioncomputing.ca/netdocuments-imanage-copilot-integration-canadian-law-firms/)
- NetDocuments MCP returns text only and states it does not replicate content into external AI stores (reduces secondary lock-in from AI layers) — [MCP via ndConnect](https://support.netdocuments.com/s/article/MCP-via-ndConnect)
- Epona365 and Jylo/Hempsons keep data in the firm's own Microsoft tenant, which avoids a vendor-hosted store — [NALA Epona](https://resources.nala.org/company/epona); [Hempsons release](https://www.britishlegalitforum.com/news/12538/)

### Inferences
- A SharePoint-native DMS (Epona365) or iManage/NetDocuments plus a disciplined metadata schema (SALI-aligned, as NetDocuments now advertises) minimises lock-in; AI-layer vendors generally do not hold the master copy, so switching them is cheaper than switching the DMS.

### Gaps
- No documentation found on exporting Legal Context Graph relationships, Smart Answers/Insight+ indexes, or Harvey/Legora/DeepJudge-generated enrichments; assume they are not portable.
- iManage's own export/offboarding terms could not be read (site not fetchable in this session).
