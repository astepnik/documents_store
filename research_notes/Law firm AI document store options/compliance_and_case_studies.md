# Law-firm AI document store (email + SharePoint + file-share indexing, embeddings, agent search): regulatory / professional-ethics constraints and documented firm experiences (state as of November 2026)

**Method note for the report writer.** Research was done via web search only; in this session every attempt to open primary PDFs/pages (ccbe.eu, sra.org.uk, lawsociety.org.uk, brak.de, kslaw.com, gibsondunn.com) failed because external hosts are blocked, so quotations from primary bar/regulator texts below come via search-engine excerpts of those documents or via reputable secondary commentary (law-firm client alerts, legal press). ## Key question 1 — GDPR: lawful basis, minimisation, retention/erasure vs. vector indexes, DPIA, processor agreements, international transfers

### Takeaway
No EU regulator has published guidance squarely on "a law firm indexing all client email into a vector store", but the pieces exist: the German DSK issued a dedicated RAG guidance paper (Nov 2025), the EDPB (Opinion 28/2024) set a high bar for calling anything AI-related "anonymous", and practitioner consensus is that chunks + embeddings + metadata in a vector store are personal data that must be deleted (not merely filtered) on erasure. The EU-US DPF survived the General Court (Sept 2025) but is under appeal at the CJEU with no hearing before late 2026, so US-hosted SaaS is lawful today but carries live "Schrems III" risk.

### Cited Findings

**Regulator guidance that applies to RAG / AI search**
- The German Datenschutzkonferenz (DSK) published "Künstliche Intelligenz und Datenschutz" v1.0 on 6 May 2024 as a checklist mainly for controllers selecting, implementing and using AI applications; focus is LLMs but applies to other AI — [DSK PDF (Bavarian DPA mirror)](https://www.datenschutz-bayern.de/dsbk-ent/DSK_OH_KI_und_Datenschutz.pdf); [DSK press release 6 May 2024](https://datenschutzkonferenz-online.de/media/pm/2024_05_06_DSK_PM_OH_KI_und_Datenschutz.pdf). (older but still current baseline)
- Commentary on the 2024 DSK paper highlights recommended measures: naming responsible persons, clear internal rules, involving works councils, carrying out DPIAs, prohibiting private accounts/devices; a DPIA under Art. 35 is required where AI use poses a high risk, with the DSK "must-DPIA" list as a guide — [Haufe](https://www.haufe.de/recht/weitere-rechtsgebiete/wirtschaftsrecht/orientierungshilfe-der-dsk-ki-und-datenschutz_210_631860.html); [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/de-de/out-law/nachrichten/datenschutzkonferenz-veroffentlicht-orientierungshilfe-zum-einsatz-von-ki). One law firm judged it "only moderately successful" with little detail — [Lausen](https://lausen.com/datenschutz-und-ki-dsk-veroeffentlicht-orientierungshilfe/).
- In November 2025 the DSK published an "Orientierungshilfe" specifically on RAG methods in generative AI systems (v1.0), its third AI paper since 2024, plus a companion paper on technical and organisational measures for developing/operating AI systems — [DSK OH RAG PDF (Berlin DPA mirror)](https://www.datenschutz-berlin.de/fileadmin/user_upload/pdf/publikationen/DSK/orientierungshilfen/2025-DSK-OH_RAG.pdf); [DSK press release (Bremen mirror)](https://www.datenschutz.bremen.de/sixcms/media.php/13/DSK_PM_OH-RAG-Systeme.pdf); [Bavarian DPA mirror](https://datenschutz-bayern.de/technik/orient/oh_RAG.pdf); commentary: [Taylor Wessing, "Mehr Datenschutz mit RAG", March 2026](https://www.taylorwessing.com/de/insights-and-events/insights/2026/03/mehr-datenschutz-mit-rag). (I could not open the RAG paper; its specific recommendations are a gap — see below.)
- The EDPS issued revised "Orientations on generative AI" (Version 2) on 28 October 2025 for EU institutions, described as more detailed guidance reflecting monitoring experience, with sections on determining roles/responsibilities and whether a GenAI system processes personal data — [EDPS revised orientations PDF](https://www.edps.europa.eu/system/files/2025-10/25-10_28_revised_genai_orientations_en.pdf); the June 2024 first version was technology-neutral and "without prejudice to the AI Act" — [EDPS 2024 PDF](https://www.edps.europa.eu/system/files/2024-05/24-05-29_genai_orientations_en_0.pdf) (older). Binding only on EU institutions, but widely used as a reference.
- CNIL's "fiches pratiques IA" (2024–2025) address the development phase; CNIL states legitimate interest is the most common basis for AI system development subject to sufficient guarantees, and that a model may fall under GDPR if it memorises personal training data — [CNIL fiches pratiques IA](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/les-fiches-pratiques-ia); [CNIL recommendations page](https://cnil.fr/fr/developpement-systemes-dia-les-recommandations-de-la-cnil-pour-respecter-le-rgpd). A third-party French site claims CNIL favours on-premise deployment for RAG over sensitive/voluminous knowledge bases and logging of queries and retrieved documents — [donneespersonnelles.fr](https://www.donneespersonnelles.fr/rag-rgpd-conformite) — **unverified against CNIL's own pages; treat as secondary opinion**.

**Are embeddings / vector stores personal data? (erasure consequences)**
- EDPB Opinion 28/2024 (Dec 2024): whether an AI model is anonymous must be assessed case by case; the threshold is high — a model is anonymous only if it is unlikely (1) to directly or indirectly identify individuals whose data was used and (2) to extract such data through queries; controllers claiming anonymity without documentation risk non-compliance; Art. 6(1)(f) cannot be the "by default" basis — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/int/legal-updates/edpb-opinion-28-2024-key-takeaways-on-processing-personal-data-in-the-context-of-ai-models); [Clyde & Co, March 2025](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/03/artificial-intelligence-and-data-protection-the-ed); [Hunton](https://hunton.com/privacy-and-information-security-law/edpb-publishes-opinion-on-processing-of-personal-data-in-the-context-of-ai-models). (The Opinion is about model weights, not RAG indexes.)
- The Hamburg DPA discussion paper (15 July 2024) argued LLMs do not "store" personal data and that embeddings are "merely mathematical representations" of trained input; the paper acknowledges extraction attacks but says presence of personal data in outputs is not conclusive evidence of memorisation — [HmbBfDI paper](https://datenschutz-hamburg.de/fileadmin/user_upload/HmbBfDI/Datenschutz/Informationen/240715_Discussion_Paper_Hamburg_DPA_KI_Models.pdf) (older); the view is contested — [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/do-llms-store-personal-data-this-is-asking-the-wrong-question); [Vischer](https://www.vischer.com/en/knowledge/blog/part-21-the-right-of-access-to-large-language-models). Again this concerns model weights, **not** a RAG vector store whose entries are literal text chunks with metadata.
- Practitioner/engineering sources (no regulator text found) converge that RAG vectors are personal data: "a vector with a payload naming a person is personal data whether or not the source file survives"; inversion attacks can partially rebuild source text; Art. 17 applies to the vectors — [tianpan.co, April 2026](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/04/20/gdpr-llm-memory-erasure-vector-database); [sota.io, 2026](https://sota.io/blog/eu-ai-act-agentic-ai-memory-rag-compliance-gdpr-vector-stores-2026).
- Query-time filtering does not satisfy erasure because data remains on disk; many vector indexes "mark a deletion rather than removing it" (tombstones until compaction), so the compaction window needs a defensible justification; a complete erasure must reach source object, vector + payload, derived artefacts (summaries, caches) — [mixpeek](https://mixpeek.com/guides/deleting-data-from-a-vector-index); [twig.so](https://help.twig.so/rag-scenarios-and-solutions/privacy/gdpr-compliance); [tianpan.co](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/04/20/gdpr-llm-memory-erasure-vector-database). Suggested architecture: keep an embedding-ID → source-record mapping and cascade deletes; redact/tokenise personal data before embedding where feasible — same sources. One vendor post claims "no commercially available vector database provides a provable deletion mechanism" — unverified, strong claim.

**Lawful basis for indexing client correspondence / mailboxes**
- For keyword searches of company mailboxes in internal investigations, Mayer Brown (Sept 2026) places processing under Art. 6(1)(f) legitimate interests, with special-category data under Art. 9(2)(b) or 9(2)(f) (legal claims) plus § 22 BDSG safeguards in Germany — [Mayer Brown, Internal Investigations Part V, Sept 2026](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/09/internal-investigations-part-v-data-processing). (Investigation-specific framing, not a standing index.)
- A German enterprise-search compliance page (vendor) instead frames the basis for employee data as Art. 6(1)(b) + § 26 BDSG where the system genuinely serves the employment relationship, and states German firms must consult the works council (Betriebsrat) before deploying enterprise search touching employee data — [compound.law (vendor)](https://compound.law/en-DE/compliance/enterprise-search-gdpr/).
- ICO guidance on the Art. 6(1)(f) three-part test (purpose, necessity, balancing) — [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/guide-to-dp/guide-to-the-uk-gdpr/legitimate-interests/what-is-the-legitimate-interests-basis).
- EDPB's 2024 legitimate-interest guidelines consultation drew criticism of the "dual requirement" that Art. 6(1)(f) must also be met when an Art. 9(2) exception applies — [TÜSİAD submission to EDPB](https://edpb.europa.eu/sites/default/files/webform/public_consultation_reply/tusiad_opinion-on-guidelines-on-processing-of-personal-data-based-on-article-6-1-f-gdpr-version-1.0.pdf); [Korff submission](https://edpb.europa.eu/sites/default/files/webform/public_consultation_reply/korff-gdpr-sensitive-data-legitimate-interest-ai-241016.pdf).

### Inferences
- A RAG index over client email is a new processing operation (new copies, new derived data, new access paths and an AI component) and plainly meets several Art. 35 / WP248 DPIA criteria (sensitive data, large scale, innovative technology), so a DPIA should be assumed mandatory in every EU jurisdiction and under UK GDPR.
- Legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)) is the realistic basis for indexing matter files and client correspondence for the firm's own service delivery; Art. 9(2)(f) covers special-category content only insofar as tied to legal claims/advice, which is a reasonable fit for matter files but a weak fit for a blanket index of all mailboxes (e.g. HR or marketing mail). Scope the crawler to matter-related repositories.
- Because vector entries are text chunks with metadata, the Hamburg "no personal data in models" reasoning cannot be relied upon for the index; build hard-delete (not soft-delete) with compaction and cascade to caches/summaries, and tie retention of chunks to the retention of the source document (the index must never outlive the original).
- For US-headquartered SaaS, structure contracts so that a DPF annulment does not break the deployment: EU-region hosting plus SCCs already in the DPA, and a documented TIA.

### Gaps
- Could not read the DSK RAG Orientierungshilfe (Nov 2025) itself; its concrete recommendations (lawful basis, logging, access control, erasure cascade) should be checked at the Berlin/Bavarian DPA links above.
- No regulator text located that explicitly says "embeddings in a vector store are personal data"; the point rests on engineering commentary plus general GDPR definitions.
- No EDPB or national DPA guidance found that is specific to law firms indexing client correspondence.
- Could not confirm the exact CJEU appeal filing date (conflicting: 31 Oct 2025 vs Jan 2026) or current Azure OpenAI abuse-monitoring retention in the 2026 Product Terms.

## Key question 2 — Professional secrecy / privilege rules: Poland, Germany, UK, CCBE (US briefly). Third-party AI/embedding APIs vs local hosting

### Takeaway
Every bar body reviewed permits cloud/AI providers in principle but conditions it on (a) contractual confidentiality binding the provider, (b) no training on client data, (c) due-diligence on hosting location — with Germany's § 43e BRAO the strictest (textform contract with statutory minimum content, "comparable" secrecy protection for non-EU providers) and the CCBE expressly naming local/firm-controlled hosting as a technical safeguard. Poland's bars moved in 2025–2026 (KIRP recommendations May 2025; NRA Code of Ethics § 23e June 2026); the SRA's August 2026 warning notice is the UK's first formal AI notice.

### Cited Findings

**CCBE (EU-wide bars)**
- CCBE published its "Guide on the use of generative AI by lawyers" on 2 October 2025, covering risks (3.3), confidentiality (4.1), professional competence (4.2), independence (4.3) — [CCBE guide PDF](https://www.ccbe.eu/fileadmin/speciality_distribution/public/documents/IT_LAW/ITL_Guides_recommendations/EN_ITL_20251002_CCBE-guide-on-the-use-of-the-use-of-generative-AI-for-lawyers.pdf); [eucrim summary](https://eucrim.eu/news/ccbe-issues-guidance-on-generative-ai-use-in-legal-practice/); [Irish Legal News](https://irishlegal.com/articles/new-guide-for-eu-lawyers-and-law-firms-on-ai).
- Excerpts (via search index of the PDF): lawyers should understand how the provider processes data including "further use of prompts for training the AI model or sharing data with third parties"; safeguards include "contractual obligations for the GenAI provider to treat data as confidential"; lawyers should avoid entering personal or client data "unless there are appropriate safeguards in place"; the guide notes "Most, if not all, of these tools are cloud-based" and lists "setting up AI systems to run locally or within a secured environment controlled by the law firm in question" as a technical safeguard — [CCBE guide PDF](https://www.ccbe.eu/fileadmin/speciality_distribution/public/documents/IT_LAW/ITL_Guides_recommendations/EN_ITL_20251002_CCBE-guide-on-the-use-of-the-use-of-generative-AI-for-lawyers.pdf). Informing clients is recommended as good practice — [Leeds Beckett law school blog, Oct 2025](https://www.leedsbeckett.ac.uk/blogs/leeds-law-school/2025/10/ai-client-secrets-and-the-lawyers-duty-of-confidentiality/).
- CCBE "Technical guide on the use of AI tools and models by lawyers" (Edition 2026, 27 March 2026) is a companion to be read with the 2025 guide; it distinguishes fully managed vendor-hosted SaaS/API services from on-premise ("An on-premise AI model runs entirely on hardware owned and controlled by a user's organisation"), describes "a dedicated machine to use with local AI models" as "the next tier and budget option", and includes a table of sample models for local use cases plus a technical glossary — [CCBE technical guide PDF](https://www.ccbe.eu/fileadmin/speciality_distribution/public/documents/IT_LAW/ITL_Guides_recommendations/EN_ITL_20260327_CCBE-technical-guide-on-the-use-of-AI-tools-and-models-by-lawyers.pdf).
- (older) CCBE 2022 "Guide on the use of AI-based tools by lawyers": "In theory, any AI tools could work on-premise as well", so deployment model is a separate decision — [CCBE 2022 PDF](https://www.ccbe.eu/fileadmin/speciality_distribution/public/documents/IT_LAW/ITL_Reports_studies/EN_ITL_20220331_Guide-AI4L.pdf).
- Belgian OVB distinguishes "open" systems (data may be stored, used for training, accessed by provider) from "closed" systems with contractual guarantees; entering client data into open systems is incompatible with professional secrecy — [Brieflee summary (vendor blog)](https://www.brieflee.be/blog/professional-secrecy-ai-guide-belgian-lawyers).

**Germany**
- § 43e BRAO (in force since the 2022 BRAO reform) governs engaging service providers who get access to secrets; BRAK's "Hinweise zum Einsatz von künstlicher Intelligenz (KI)" (7 pages, Stand Dezember 2024) treat AI providers as § 43e service providers: the lawyer must carefully select the provider and conclude a contract at least in text form with the statutory minimum content (confidentiality obligation, instruction on criminal consequences under § 203 StGB, rules on further sub-persons); the guide also explains AI Act duties and their relation to professional law — [BRAK Leitfaden PDF](https://www.brak.de/fileadmin/service/publikationen/Handlungshinweise/BRAK_Leitfaden_mit_Hinweisen_zum_KI-Einsatz_Stand_12_2024.pdf) (mirror: [RAK Köln](https://www.rak-koeln.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Hinweise-zum-Einsatz-von-KI.pdf)); [IWW summary](https://www.iww.de/kp/berufsrecht/kuenstliche-intelligenz-leitfaden-der-brak-zum-einsatz-von-ki-in-der-beratungspraxis-f165486); [DATEV Magazin, Jan 2025](https://www.datev-magazin.de/nachrichten-steuern-recht/recht/kuenstliche-intelligenz-in-anwaltskanzleien-brak-veroeffentlicht-leitfaden-135422); [Myra Security summary](https://www.myrasecurity.com/de/ratgeber/ki-kanzlei-paragraf-203-stgb/).
- Per the same summaries of the BRAK guide: for providers abroad, § 43e Abs. 4 BRAO requires that secrecy protection there be comparable to Germany's; cloud/LLM providers are "frequently US-based" and whether US providers meet this is "not conclusively settled"; mere removal of names/addresses is regularly insufficient anonymisation if matter information is derivable from context; there is no duty to use AI and no general duty to inform clients of AI use, but transparency is recommended; abstract queries without matter reference carry lower risk; § 43e Abs. 5 consent duties may apply where there is direct single-matter reference; the lawyer retains final responsibility for output (§ 43 BRAO) — [IWW](https://www.iww.de/kp/berufsrecht/kuenstliche-intelligenz-leitfaden-der-brak-zum-einsatz-von-ki-in-der-beratungspraxis-f165486); [Myra](https://www.myrasecurity.com/de/ratgeber/ki-kanzlei-paragraf-203-stgb/); [compound.law (vendor)](https://compound.law/de-DE/compliance/43e-brao-ki/). One source notes the guide has not been updated since Dec 2024.
- BRAK's older cloud guidance "Hinweise zum Umgang mit Office 365 / Cloud" (Stand 18 Jan 2023) — [BRAK PDF](https://WWW.BRAK.DE/fileadmin/service/publikationen/Handlungshinweise/Hinweise_zum_Umgang_mit_Office_365_Cloud_Stand_2023-01-18.pdf) (older). Trade press: "largely agreed that cloud services are permissible under the conditions of § 43e BRAO" — [Anwaltspraxis Magazin](https://anwaltspraxis-magazin.de/kanzleimagazin/von-datenschutz-bis-ethik-ki-guidelines-fuer-die-kanzleiwelt/).
- Deutscher Anwaltverein (DAV) Initiativ-Stellungnahme Nr. 32/2025 (July 2025) on AI in the legal profession: confidentiality under § 43a Abs. 2 BRAO and § 203 StGB is central; use of external cloud/AI providers is "in principle permissible" under conditions (written confidentiality obligation, necessity principle, § 43e instruction on criminal consequences); DAV concludes the requirements can be met and sees "no serious hurdles" in professional law or data protection if rules are followed — [DAV SN 32/25](https://anwaltverein.de/newsroom/sn-32-25-einsatz-von-ki-in-der-anwaltschaft); [beck-aktuell, 24 July 2025](https://www.beck-aktuell.de/rechtsbranche/anwaltschaft/dav-anwaltverein-ki-einsatz-kanzleien-chancen-risiken-anforderungen-2025-07-24); [Otto Schmidt ZKM-Report](https://www.otto-schmidt.de/blog/zkm-report/dav-liefert-orientierungshilfe-zum-ki-einsatz-in-der-anwaltschaft-ZKMR0001701.html). A later DAV paper proposes a "bridge/blueprint" for legally secure AI use under AI Act + GDPR — [beck-aktuell](https://www.beck-aktuell.de/daily/meldung/detail/dav-rechtssicherheit-ki-einsatz-kanzleien-ki-vo-dsgvo-bridge-blueprint-datenschutz).
- beck-aktuell (Nico Kuhlmann, 19 Aug 2025) argues locally run open-weight models are "part of the solution" to the professional-law problem because they run "under one's own responsibility on one's own IT infrastructure" and offline, while conceding commercial models "will always be better"; large models need special hardware; tools named: Ollama, LM Studio — [beck-aktuell](https://www.beck-aktuell.de/rechtsbranche/legal-tech-digitalisierung/ki-kanzlei-open-weight-modelle-berufsrecht-2025-08-19). A May 2026 NJW forum piece describes a guide distinguishing locally installed/self-hosted, self-hosted in a colocation data centre, and fully managed models, with separate treatment of open-weight models — [beck-aktuell, 19 May 2026](https://www.beck-aktuell.de/aus-der-njw/forum/technisches-fuer-die-anwaltschaft-2026-05-19). Open-weight licences vary and must be checked for commercial use — [GvW, Aug 2025](https://www.gvw.com/aktuelles/blog/detail/was-sie-beim-self-hosting-von-llms-wissen-muessen).
- CLOUD Act concern: a vendor blog argues an EU region does not protect against US parent-company access — [WZ-IT (vendor)](https://wz-it.com/knowledge/ki/lokale-ki-berufsgeheimnis/) — opinion, not bar guidance.

**Poland**
- KIRP (radcowie prawni): "Rekomendacje dotyczące korzystania przez radców prawnych z narzędzi opartych na sztucznej inteligencji", presented 13 May 2025 at a conference organised by KIRP, Microsoft Polska and Sołtysiński Kawecki & Szlęzak; in force from 28 May 2025; structured as preparation / implementation / use, with a chapter on benefits, challenges, risks and a review of foreign bar guidance; explicitly "not final" — [KIRP PDF](https://kirp.pl/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/REKOMENDACJE-DOTYCZACE-KORZYSTANIA-PRZEZ-RADCOW-PRAWNYCH-Z-NARZEDZI-OPARTYCH-NA-SZTUCZNEJ-INTELIGENCJI.pdf); [KIRP launch page](https://kirp.pl/premiera-rekomendacji-kirp-dotyczacych-korzystania-z-ai-przez-radcow-prawnych/); [KIRP recommendations page](https://kirp.pl/rekomendacje-dotyczace-korzystania-z-ai/); [OIRP Kraków (in-force date)](https://oirp.krakow.pl/pdf/rekomendacjekorzystania-z-narzedzi-ai-przez-radcow-prawnych/); English version — [belgium.pl mirror](https://belgium.pl/site/assets/files/19871/rekomendacje_ai_eng.pdf).

### Inferences
- For an internal RAG store the professional-secrecy analysis turns on *who can see the data*: a firm-controlled store (on-prem or firm-tenant cloud) with a non-training, zero-retention embedding/LLM endpoint maps onto the CCBE "closed/secured environment", the DAV "permissible with conditions" view and the SRA "secure environment, no training" language. A consumer or shared-tenant API without contractual confidentiality fails all of them.
- Germany is the binding constraint for a multi-jurisdiction design: every provider in the chain (vector DB SaaS, embedding API, LLM API, connectors) needs a § 43e-compliant textform agreement, and a US provider needs documented reasoning on "comparable protection" — which is why German commentary pushes EU-sovereign or self-hosted options more than UK commentary does.
- Poland now has explicit bar-level texts (KIRP 2025, NRA § 23e 2026) which reference local processing of sensitive data and provider-contract verification; a Polish firm should map its DPIA and vendor contracts to those recommendations.

## Key question 3 — EU AI Act: does a law-firm document search / RAG system attract obligations in 2026?

### Takeaway
An internal document-search/RAG tool is not an Annex III high-risk use, so the main AI Act touchpoints are Article 50 transparency (in force 2 August 2026, applies to deployers regardless of risk class) and Article 4 AI literacy (in force since Feb 2025, softened by the Digital Omnibus); the Omnibus moved Annex III high-risk deadlines to 2 December 2027 and Annex I to 2 August 2028, which is irrelevant to a search tool but matters if the firm later adds e.g. employment-screening or credit-related agents.

### Cited Findings
- Digital Omnibus on AI: standalone Annex III high-risk obligations deferred from 2 Aug 2026 to 2 Dec 2027 (16 months); Annex I product-embedded high-risk to 2 Aug 2028; rationale is delayed harmonised standards — [Cooley](https://cdp.cooley.com/digital-ai-omnibus-delays-key-deadlines-introduces-new-rules/); [Holistic AI](https://www.holisticai.com/blog/eu-ai-acts-deadline-moved-2027); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act).
- Adoption timeline (sources partly conflict): Council final approval reported 29 June 2026 — [SecurePrivacy](https://secureprivacy.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-digital-omnibus-the-new-high-risk-ai-deadlines-after-council-approval); "enters into force" reported 3 Aug 2026 — [K&L Gates via Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/eu-digital-omnibus-on-ai-enters-into-force/1825722); Sept 2026 coverage confirms the delay applies to high-risk rules only, "not the rulebook itself" — [FinTech Global, 2 Sept 2026](https://fintech.global/2026/09/02/brussels-delays-high-risk-ai-rules-not-the-rulebook-itself/). Gibson Dunn had warned the deferral only works if enacted before 2 Aug 2026.
- GPAI model rules already in effect remain; Article 50 transparency duties stayed on the original 2 August 2026 date — [Cooley](https://cdp.cooley.com/digital-ai-omnibus-delays-key-deadlines-introduces-new-rules/); [Goodwin, Aug 2026](https://goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/alerts-technology-dpc-eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-now-in-force).
- Article 50 is not tied to the risk classification; paragraphs 1–2 bind providers at design time, 3–4 bind deployers at use time; for an internal tool the relevant duty is informing people they interact with an AI system; the Commission adopted Article 50 guidelines and (June 2026) a Code of Practice on transparency of AI-generated content; fines up to EUR 15m or 3% turnover — [artificialintelligenceact.eu](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/); [Stephenson Harwood](https://perspectives.stephensonharwood.com/post/102nfqh/eu-ai-act-update-european-commission-adopts-guidelines-on-article-50-transparenc); [TrustArc](https://trustarc.com/products/privacy-data-governance/nymity-research/eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-guidelines/); [Dastra](https://www.dastra.eu/en/blog/ai-act-transparency-rules-what-changes-august-2-2026/60164).
- Article 4 AI literacy: the Omnibus reportedly softened the duty from "ensure a sufficient level" to "take measures to support the development of AI literacy" (one source dates this 27 July 2026); Commission guidance says Art. 50 disclosures complement but do not replace Art. 4 — [Lumenova, Sept 2026](https://www.lumenova.ai/blog/eu-ai-act-article-50-article-5/); [Dastra](https://www.dastra.eu/en/blog/ai-act-transparency-rules-what-changes-august-2-2026/60164). Secondary sources; verify in the Omnibus text.
- BRAK's Dec 2024 guide explains the AI Act's requirements and their relationship to German professional law — [DATEV Magazin](https://www.datev-magazin.de/nachrichten-steuern-recht/recht/kuenstliche-intelligenz-in-anwaltskanzleien-brak-veroeffentlicht-leitfaden-135422).

### Inferences
- A firm building its own RAG system is a "deployer" of GPAI models and "provider" of a non-high-risk AI system: practical obligations are an AI-literacy programme, transparency labelling in the UI (and where AI-generated text is passed on), and documentation; there is no conformity assessment or registration duty for search/drafting assistance. Risk classification should be re-checked whenever agents are pointed at HR, KYC/AML or litigation-prediction use cases.

### Gaps
- Official Journal citation and exact entry-into-force date of the Omnibus regulation not confirmed (sources: 29 June 2026 Council approval; 3 Aug 2026 entry into force).

## Key question 4 — Ethical walls / conflicts, document-level security, audit trails; ISO 27001 / SOC 2 and client outside-counsel guidelines on AI

### Takeaway
The documented failure mode is not hallucination but over-retrieval: Copilot and similar tools surface anything the user technically has access to, exposing years of lax SharePoint/DMS permissions; firms respond with query-time permission trimming (never index-time only), matter-level isolation, Restricted SharePoint Search, and ethical-wall products that override SharePoint ACLs. Clients and insurers increasingly require disclosure/consent, no-training clauses, audit rights and SOC 2 / ISO 27001 (and now ISO 42001) evidence.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- Design requirement: retrieval must filter by the user's *current* entitlements at query time (ACL metadata on chunks is a cache that needs near-real-time sync), ethical walls/matter screens must be enforced in the retrieval layer independently of SharePoint ACLs, and every query/retrieved-chunk/answer should be logged for audit and client-reporting (this also satisfies Hartford-style record-keeping clauses).
- Expect OCG/insurer diligence to ask for: no-training clauses, data-residency, SOC 2 Type II or ISO 27001 of every SaaS in the chain, an AI-use register per matter, and the ability to exclude a client's matters from the index entirely.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) account of a law-firm permission leak via AI search was located; the practice-group-level permission failure is described by a consultancy without naming the firm.
- Insurer-specific EU/UK panel guidelines on AI were not found (Hartford is US).

## Key question 5 — Legal hold and e-discovery: how indexing copies of email affects retention; immutable originals

### Takeaway
An index creates extra copies (chunks, embeddings, caches, Copilot prompt/response records) that can outlive or fall outside retention and hold rules; Microsoft's documentation shows that AI interaction data (prompts/responses) is itself discoverable and must be retained/held deliberately. No bar or court guidance specific to law-firm RAG indexes was found.

### Cited Findings
- Copilot prompts and responses are stored in a hidden mailbox folder searchable by Purview eDiscovery; permanent deletion is suspended while the mailbox is under Litigation Hold / eDiscovery hold or another retention policy — [Microsoft Learn: retention policies for Copilot](https://learn.microsoft.com/bs-latn-ba/purview/retention-policies-copilot); [Microsoft Tech Community: collecting Copilot data with Purview](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/t5/microsoft-security-community/collecting-microsoft-365-copilot-data-with-microsoft-purview/ba-p/4516489).
- Audit logs record that a Copilot interaction occurred but not the text; substance requires an eDiscovery search; without a retention policy scoped to Copilot, early interactions may already be gone when a hold arrives — [A Guide to Cloud](https://www.aguidetocloud.com/blog/auditing-microsoft-365-copilot/); [Microsoft Zero Trust assessment guidance](https://microsoft.github.io/zerotrustassessment/docs/workshop-guidance/AI/AI_097).
- A mailbox hold does not extend to the SharePoint Embedded site holding Copilot Pages; the site URL must be added to the hold; Copilot "memory" items reportedly are not covered by retention policies/holds — [Lighthouse](https://www.lighthouseglobal.com/blog/microsoft-copilot-fleet); [Collab365](https://spaces.collab365.com/posts/microsoft-releases-guide-for-collecting-copilot-da-DPkREn).
- Courts treat AI data under ordinary discovery rules; hold notices should name AI tools explicitly; chat tools often default to auto-delete; capture timestamps, user identity, session IDs, tool versions — [Onna](https://www.onna.com/resources/blog/preserving-ai-generated-content-legal-guide).
- Journaling captures messages in transit (broader than snapshot); backups "can be overwritten, are not indexed for search, and do not meet legal standards for immutable evidence"; holds keep items "preserved immutably and suspended from any normal retention policies" — [FindLaw e-discovery guide](https://www.findlaw.com/legal/technology/ediscovery-guide/records-management-archiving.html); [Lawgistics (vendor)](https://www.lawgistics.com/services-solutions/emailspam-protection-southern-california/email-archiving-retention-for-law-firms/); [Smarsh (vendor)](https://www.smarsh.com/platform/communications-intelligence/discovery); UK legal-hold challenges — [Kiteworks (vendor)](https://www.kiteworks.com/?p=254628).

### Inferences
- Treat the vector store as a derived, disposable index: the system of record (mailbox/journal archive, DMS) must stay immutable and under hold; the index should (a) never be the only copy, (b) inherit deletion from the source via the ID mapping (Key Q1), and (c) have an explicit rule for what happens when a source is under hold but the data subject requests erasure (hold wins; document the Art. 17(3)(e) reliance).
- Agent prompts, retrieved-chunk lists and answers are new records that may be discoverable and may contain client confidential material; decide retention for them deliberately (short for logs with content; longer for audit metadata).

### Gaps
- No court decision or bar guidance addressing discoverability of a law firm's own RAG index/embeddings was found.
- EU-specific (Polish/German) retention rules for lawyers' files (e.g. 10-year akta sprawy rules) were not researched here.

## Key question 7 — Guidance explicitly favouring local/on-prem AI, or explicitly accepting EU-hosted SaaS

### Takeaway
No bar body mandates on-prem, but CCBE (2025 guide and 2026 technical guide), German commentary around § 43e Abs. 4 BRAO, KIRP (2025) and US malpractice insurers all name local/closed/firm-controlled processing as a preferred safeguard, while DAV, BRAK and the SRA explicitly accept third-party cloud/AI providers subject to contractual confidentiality, no-training and (for Germany) comparable-protection conditions.

### Cited Findings
- Favouring local/controlled: CCBE 2025 lists running AI "locally or within a secured environment controlled by the law firm" as a technical safeguard and warns most tools are cloud-based — [CCBE guide](https://www.ccbe.eu/fileadmin/speciality_distribution/public/documents/IT_LAW/ITL_Guides_recommendations/EN_ITL_20251002_CCBE-guide-on-the-use-of-the-use-of-generative-AI-for-lawyers.pdf); CCBE 2026 technical guide gives an on-premise tier and sample local models — [CCBE technical guide](https://www.ccbe.eu/fileadmin/speciality_distribution/public/documents/IT_LAW/ITL_Guides_recommendations/EN_ITL_20260327_CCBE-technical-guide-on-the-use-of-AI-tools-and-models-by-lawyers.pdf); beck-aktuell: open-weight models on own infrastructure as part of the solution — [beck-aktuell, Aug 2025](https://www.beck-aktuell.de/rechtsbranche/legal-tech-digitalisierung/ki-kanzlei-open-weight-modelle-berufsrecht-2025-08-19); KIRP: encryption, access control and "local processing of sensitive information" — [Gazeta Prawna](https://www.gazetaprawna.pl/firma/artykuly/11193429,sztuczna-inteligencja-w-pracy-prawnika-i-ksiegowego.html); ALPS: "closed or controlled AI environments" — [Minnesota Lawyer](https://minnlawyer.com/2026/09/21/5-ways-malpractice-insurers-say-law-firms-can-reduce-ai-risk/); OVB (Belgium) closed vs open systems — [Brieflee](https://www.brieflee.be/blog/professional-secrecy-ai-guide-belgian-lawyers); CNIL allegedly favouring on-prem for sensitive RAG bases (unverified secondary) — [donneespersonnelles.fr](https://www.donneespersonnelles.fr/rag-rgpd-conformite).
- Accepting cloud/SaaS with conditions: DAV "in principle permissible" with written confidentiality and necessity — [beck-aktuell, July 2025](https://www.beck-aktuell.de/rechtsbranche/anwaltschaft/dav-anwaltverein-ki-einsatz-kanzleien-chancen-risiken-anforderungen-2025-07-24); BRAK treats AI providers as § 43e service providers (permissible with textform contract; third-country comparability test) — [IWW](https://www.iww.de/kp/berufsrecht/kuenstliche-intelligenz-leitfaden-der-brak-zum-einsatz-von-ki-in-der-beratungspraxis-f165486); German trade consensus that cloud is permissible under § 43e — [Anwaltspraxis Magazin](https://anwaltspraxis-magazin.de/kanzleimagazin/von-datenschutz-bis-ethik-ki-guidelines-fuer-die-kanzleiwelt/); SRA: data must remain in a "secure environment" and not train models "without explicit authorisation" (hosting-neutral) — [SRA](https://www.sra.org.uk/solicitors/guidance/misuse-ai/); KIRP recommendations were co-produced with Microsoft Polska, implying acceptance of hyperscaler cloud under conditions — [KIRP launch page](https://kirp.pl/premiera-rekomendacji-kirp-dotyczacych-korzystania-z-ai-przez-radcow-prawnych/).
- EU-hosting mechanics for SaaS: Microsoft EU Data Boundary / regional Azure OpenAI endpoints with opt-in for cross-region movement — [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-copilot-service/copilot-data-movement-geos); zero-retention not self-service — [IntuitionLabs](https://intuitionlabs.ai/insights/guides/zero-data-retention-copilot-azure-openai-life-sciences).

### Inferences
- A defensible pan-EU architecture is: firm-controlled store and vector index (on-prem or firm's own EU cloud tenant), embeddings/LLM via either local open-weight models or an EU-region enterprise API with contractual no-training + zero/limited retention + § 43e-style confidentiality clause, with an architecture note explaining the § 43e Abs. 4 comparability reasoning for any US-parented provider.
- Explicit "EU-hosted SaaS is fine" statements from bars were not found; acceptance is implicit (conditions-based) rather than a safe harbour.

### Gaps
- No bar guidance located that explicitly addresses the CLOUD Act / US-parent question for AI providers beyond the BRAK "not conclusively settled" remark via secondary sources.
- No Polish bar text on hosting location for AI tools found.
